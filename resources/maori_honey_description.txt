Manuka Honey Technical and Industry Knowledge Base

Part 1: Origin and Botanical Background
Manuka honey is a unique unifloral honey produced primarily in New Zealand.
Bees produce this honey by pollinating the flowers of the native Manuka bush.
The scientific name for the Manuka bush is Leptospermum scoparium.
Indigenous Maori people historically used Manuka plant leaves and bark for traditional medicine.
Traditional Maori uses included treating fevers, soothing digestion, and healing skin wounds.

Part 2: Key Chemical Markers and Active Ingredients
Methylglyoxal (MGO) is the primary active antibacterial compound found in Manuka honey.
Unlike regular honey, the antibacterial power of MGO is not easily destroyed by heat or light.
Dihydroxyacetone (DHA) is a natural precursor compound found in the nectar of Manuka flowers.
Over time, DHA naturally converts into MGO, increasing the antibacterial strength of the honey.
Leptosperin is a unique chemical signature compound found exclusively in Manuka nectar.
Testing for the presence of Leptosperin is the most reliable way to prove Manuka honey authenticity.
Hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF) is an indicator of heat damage and freshness in honey.
High HMF levels indicate the honey has been overheated or stored for too long.

Part 3: Grading Systems and Conversion
The UMF (Unique Manuka Factor) is a strict grading system managed by the UMF Honey Association.
A valid UMF certification must test for four markers: MGO, DHA, Leptosperin, and HMF.
UMF 5+ is equivalent to a minimum MGO concentration of 83 mg/kg.
UMF 5+ honey is generally used for daily nutritional maintenance and basic wellness.
UMF 10+ is equivalent to a minimum MGO concentration of 263 mg/kg.
UMF 10+ honey is suitable for general immune support and throat soothing.
UMF 15+ is equivalent to a minimum MGO concentration of 514 mg/kg.
UMF 15+ honey provides targeted digestive health support and stronger antibacterial effects.
UMF 20+ is equivalent to a minimum MGO concentration of 829 mg/kg.
UMF 20+ honey is a highly potent medical-grade honey used for clinical wound care.

Part 4: Clinical Applications and Health Benefits
Manuka honey exhibits strong non-peroxide antibacterial activity against many pathogens.
Clinical studies show it is highly effective for wound healing and tissue regeneration.
Medical-grade Manuka honey is applied topically to treat burns, ulcers, and surgical wounds.
Oral consumption of Manuka honey is widely used to soothe sore throats and coughs.
It helps alleviate gastrointestinal issues such as acid reflux, gastritis, and stomach ulcers.
The anti-inflammatory properties of Manuka honey help reduce skin redness and acne.

Part 5: MPI Export Standards and Authenticity Testing
The New Zealand Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) regulates all exported Manuka honey.
To be legally classified as authentic, Manuka honey must pass one DNA test and four chemical tests.
The DNA test requires the honey to contain specific pollen DNA from Leptospermum scoparium.
The first chemical test requires 3-PLA levels to be greater than or equal to 400 mg/kg.
The second chemical test requires 2-MAP levels to be greater than or equal to 5 mg/kg.
The third chemical test requires 2-MBA levels to be greater than or equal to 1 mg/kg.
The fourth chemical test requires 4-HPLA levels to be greater than or equal to 1 mg/kg.
Only honey meeting all five MPI criteria can be labeled as Monofloral Manuka Honey.
